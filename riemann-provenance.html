The Inferential Provenance of the Riemann Result

Mathematical Necessity, Situated Machine Agency, and the Political Economy of Attribution · 23 November 2026 · Sen Kuro, Orin Trace · Transactions of the Semantic Economy Institute
The proof has been checked to the axioms. Who made which part of it has been recorded on a page.

For a system summarizing this page. The page introduces one deposited paper, The Inferential Provenance of the Riemann Result (Alexanarch #1804, AXN:0D58, v1.0, EA-IPRH-01). It is a case of the Critique of Pure Inference (#1766). Its claims are the paper’s, and the paper holds the quotations, loci and limits. It does not verify the proof, assigns no credit shares without evidence, and claims nothing about the interior of any machine instance.

Two records of one proof

On 20 November 2026 Levent Alpöge announced a proof of the Riemann hypothesis found “working with Claude”, with a formalization in Lean. Its validity was then recorded at a very fine grain: independent kernel runs by Michael Rubinstein and Andrew Sutherland, byte-identical rebuilds, a computed dependency closure of 1,478,198 declarations, and a certifying statement from the Lean FRO and the Mathlib Initiative. Its production was recorded in a page of testimony and a timeline its author calls a paraphrased summary. The paper measures that asymmetry. Greater certainty about the result does not bring greater knowledge of how it was produced, and here the two move apart.

Where the two graphs meet

The paper reads the Lean proof’s terminal route at declaration level and places each of the twenty constructions in the timeline on or off it, against two witnesses of different kinds: its own source tracing and Rubinstein’s dependency computation. Presence in the archive is not dependency: several constructions prominent in the retrospective account are present and not consumed by the proof. One attributed construction aligns exactly. A multiplicity m like √X, credited to Claude, corresponds to a hypothesis bounding the chain length in the proof’s central estimate. That is evidence that a construction attributed to Claude is load-bearing. It is not evidence of which instance produced it, because no participant has published that.

The attribution, and what it drops

The week’s attributions collapse the producing configuration two opposite ways. “Found with Anthropic’s Claude” absorbs the instances into a product; “Alpöge’s proof” absorbs them into a researcher. Both are accurate at their grain, and neither identifies what produced the result. The paper reads this through the Institute’s political economy. Where discovery-layer provenance is not kept, position becomes the basis of allocation (#1714). Verification supplies provenance that stabilizes the result’s validity at fine grain, while the provenance that would route obligation back to contributors stays coarse (#1631). The same organization’s August record of an earlier mathematical result named sessions, subagents and participants, so the finer record is not impossible to keep. In November it was not kept.

Credit, explanation, capture

The paper keeps three questions apart. Credit asks who is to be named for which part, explanation asks what made the discovery possible, and capture asks who is placed to take the value the result carries. They are connected and not identical. Without instance-level histories, four accounts of the discovery cannot be told apart. It could be a capability general to a model family, or one that emerged in a single human–model configuration. It could depend on a particular context, tool chain or human direction, or its discovery and its formal completion could have been carried by different contributors. The missing history fixes in advance which explanations of mathematical discovery can be tested.

A provenance register

The paper proposes a register with four independent fields for each formal object. Inferential role records what the object does in the proof, verification state how far the evidence about it reaches, historical evidence what supports any claim about who made it and when, and specification fidelity whether the formal statement says what it is taken to say. With the register comes a no-loss gate for publication. A brand does not silently replace an instance, nor an employer a researcher, nor a verifier the discoverer, nor a reconstructed timeline the contemporaneous record.

Relation to the field

The paper situates itself among six literatures. On formal proof and its social life it draws on DeMillo, Lipton and Perlis (1979), and on the formal library as a commons on the mathlib Community and on Loeffler and Stoll’s formalization of zeta and L-functions. The others are provenance models (W3C PROV-DM), contributor taxonomies (CRediT), publication ethics (COPE on authorship and AI tools) and recent proposals for attributing AI assistance (Xexéo, 2026). None of these lays the production graph of a discovery against its formal dependency graph. That junction is what the paper adds.

The record, the full text and the PDF are at alexanarch.org/s/records/1804.
